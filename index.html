About Me

I am a Master's student in Urban Planning (Smart City & Big Data program) at Peking University, and currently work as a Research Assistant in the Data Science Department at Duke Kunshan University. I completed my Bachelor's degree at Beijing Jiaotong University (Major in Traffic Engineering; Minor in Finance) graduating as a Beijing Outstanding Graduate.

My research lies at the intersection of Urban Spatiotemporal Big Data and Artificial Intelligence (Geo-AI). I am highly passionate about harnessing advanced methodologies—such as **Large Language Models (LLMs), Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), Causal Inference**, and **Mathematical Optimization**—to design accessible, sustainable, and reliable smart transport systems and cognitive neighborhoods.

Research Focus Areas

Smart Mobility & Autonomous Vehicles

Integrating Shared Autonomous Vehicles (SAVs) with traditional public transit, balancing system-wide travel efficiency with personalized user equilibrium routing.

Spatiotemporal Big Data Mining

Developing Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), dual-stream BiLSTMs, and robust semi-supervised frameworks (such as ST-ProC) for traffic state prediction and trajectory classification.

Large Language Models (Geo-AI)

Leveraging LLMs for structured causal text extraction and building RAG pipelines (such as AskNearby) that model spatial familiarity & cognitive maps.

Causal Inference & Policy Analysis

Applying quasi-experiments, Difference-in-Differences (DID), and Interrupted Time Series models on signaling data to quantify post-adoption EV shifts and spatial sentiment policy impacts.

Joint Design of Public Transit and Autonomous Vehicle Mobility Systems for Tourist Cities Considering Abnormal Trips

Shi, Z., Luo, S., Niu, L., Shao, C.

Transportation Research Part C: Emerging Technologies

DOI
Key Contribution: Formulated an integrated Shared Autonomous Vehicles (SAV)-transit design problem accommodating heterogeneous demand types (normal trips with carpooling and private tour trips requiring privacy), balancing system efficiency and accessibility in tourist city contexts. Used continuum approximation and network queuing theory to derive flow equilibrium equations, minimizing total agency and user costs to find optimal SAV fleet size and FRT networks. Completed a 15-25% cost reduction.

Event-CausNet: Unlocking Causal Knowledge from Text with Large Language Models for Reliable Spatio-Temporal Forecasting

Niu, L., Wang, Z., Guan, S., Sun, P.

Under Review at IEEE Intelligent Vehicles Symposium (IEEE IV 2026)

arXiv
Key Contribution: Overcomes limits of spatio-temporal GNNs during non-recurring anomalies (accidents, work zones) by building a causal inference framework using LLMs to extract causal knowledge.
